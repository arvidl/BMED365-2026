# Differential Diagnosis - Prompt Templates

These templates demonstrate structured approaches to using LLMs for differential diagnosis support.

> **IMPORTANT**: These are educational examples only. Never use AI for actual clinical diagnosis without proper validation and physician oversight.

---

## 1. Structured Differential Diagnosis

```
ROLE: You are a clinical reasoning assistant helping medical students learn differential diagnosis.

PATIENT PRESENTATION:
- Age: 45-year-old male
- Chief complaint: Sudden onset severe headache
- Duration: Started 2 hours ago
- Character: "Worst headache of my life"
- Associated symptoms: Neck stiffness, photophobia, nausea
- Vital signs: BP 165/95, HR 88, Temp 37.8°C
- Medical history: Hypertension, smoking 20 pack-years

TASK: Generate a differential diagnosis with the following structure:

1. **Most Likely Diagnoses** (top 3):
   - Diagnosis name
   - Key supporting findings
   - Key findings that would argue against

2. **Must Not Miss** (life-threatening conditions):
   - Condition
   - Why it must be considered
   - Initial workup to rule out

3. **Other Considerations**:
   - Less likely but possible diagnoses

4. **Recommended Initial Workup**:
   - Prioritized list of investigations

FORMAT: Use clinical reasoning language appropriate for medical education.
```

## 2. Symptom-Based DDx Template

```
You are helping a medical student work through a differential diagnosis.

PRESENTING SYMPTOM: [Insert symptom, e.g., "Chest pain"]

Please provide:

A) **Organ System Approach**:
   - Cardiac causes (list with brief distinguishing features)
   - Pulmonary causes
   - Gastrointestinal causes
   - Musculoskeletal causes
   - Other (psychiatric, referred pain, etc.)

B) **Red Flags** that require immediate evaluation:
   - List with rationale

C) **Key History Questions** to narrow the differential:
   - Numbered list of questions with what each helps distinguish

D) **Physical Exam Findings** to look for:
   - Organized by system
```

## 3. Pattern Recognition Exercise

```
ROLE: Clinical educator

CLINICAL PATTERN:
Young woman with:
- Fatigue
- Joint pain (symmetric, small joints)
- Malar rash
- Photosensitivity
- Oral ulcers

TASK:
1. What syndrome/diagnosis does this pattern suggest?
2. What additional history would you seek?
3. What laboratory tests would you order?
4. What are the diagnostic criteria for this condition?
5. What conditions can mimic this presentation?

NOTE: Include reasoning for each step to support learning.
```

## 4. Emergency Triage DDx

```
CONTEXT: Emergency department, rapid assessment needed.

PATIENT: 68-year-old woman with acute dyspnea

VITAL SIGNS:
- SpO2: 88% on room air
- RR: 28/min
- HR: 110/min
- BP: 90/60 mmHg
- Temp: 38.2°C

TASK: Time-sensitive differential diagnosis

1. **Immediate Life Threats** (must rule out in first 10 minutes):
   - List with key discriminating features

2. **Urgent Conditions** (evaluate within 1 hour):
   - List with workup priorities

3. **First Actions**:
   - What to do while awaiting test results

4. **If unstable** - What changes the approach?
```

## 5. Pediatric DDx Template

```
ROLE: Pediatric clinical reasoning assistant

PATIENT: 3-year-old boy
PRESENTING: High fever (39.5°C) for 3 days, rash appeared today

RASH DESCRIPTION:
- Started on trunk, spreading to extremities
- Maculopapular, blanching
- Child appears ill but consolable

ADDITIONAL FINDINGS:
- Irritable
- Reduced oral intake
- Lymphadenopathy (cervical)
- No cough or coryza initially

TASK: Pediatric-specific differential diagnosis

1. **Viral Exanthems** to consider:
   - Classic features of each

2. **Bacterial Causes** not to miss:
   - Red flags for each

3. **Kawasaki Disease**: Does this meet criteria? What criteria are present/absent?

4. **When to worry**: Signs requiring immediate intervention

5. **Parental counseling**: What to tell the family
```

## 7. Self-Assessment DDx Exercise

```
SCENARIO: 55-year-old man with 2 weeks of progressive weakness in legs

Before reading further, write down your top 3 differential diagnoses.

---

ADDITIONAL INFORMATION (reveal after initial list):
- Weakness started distally, now proximal
- Areflexia on examination
- Recent URI 3 weeks ago
- Sensory symptoms minimal
- No bladder/bowel issues

Now revise your differential:
1. What diagnosis moved to the top?
2. What made you change your ranking?
3. What single test would you order first?
4. What treatment might be needed urgently?

[AI should not provide answer until student attempts first]
```

---

## Usage Notes for Students

1. **Always verify** AI-generated differentials against reliable sources
2. **Practice first** - try generating your own DDx before using AI
3. **Use as learning tool** - understand the reasoning, don't just memorize lists
4. **Clinical context matters** - AI cannot examine patients or understand nuance
5. **Discuss with supervisors** - AI is for learning, not clinical decisions

## Discussion Questions

1. In what ways might AI-assisted DDx be helpful for medical students?
2. What are the risks of relying too heavily on AI for diagnostic reasoning?
3. How might AI DDx tools change medical education?
4. What role should AI play in actual clinical practice?
