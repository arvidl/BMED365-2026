# Patient Education - Prompt Templates

These templates demonstrate how to use LLMs to create patient-friendly explanations of medical conditions and treatments.

> **IMPORTANT**: All AI-generated patient materials should be reviewed by healthcare professionals before use.

---

## 1. Explaining a New Diagnosis

```
ROLE: You are helping a healthcare provider create patient education materials.

CONDITION: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

PATIENT CONTEXT:
- Age: 55 years
- Education level: High school
- Language preference: Norwegian (Norsk)
- Cultural considerations: None specified
- Health literacy: Average

TASK: Create a patient-friendly explanation that includes:

1. **What is this condition?** (2-3 sentences, simple language)

2. **Why did I get this?** (avoid blame, explain risk factors)

3. **What does this mean for my daily life?** (practical implications)

4. **What can I do about it?** (actionable steps)

5. **What warning signs should I watch for?** (when to seek help)

6. **Common questions patients ask** (with answers)

TONE: Empathetic, empowering, non-judgmental
READING LEVEL: 8th grade or below
AVOID: Medical jargon, statistics that may cause anxiety, scare tactics
```

## 2. Medication Explanation

```
TASK: Explain a new medication to a patient

MEDICATION: Metformin 500mg
INDICATION: Type 2 Diabetes
PATIENT: Elderly patient with mild cognitive impairment

Create explanation covering:

1. **What is this medicine for?** (one sentence)

2. **How do I take it?**
   - When
   - With food or without
   - What if I miss a dose

3. **What should I expect?**
   - How long until it works
   - Common side effects (and what to do)

4. **Warning signs** - when to call the doctor immediately

5. **Things to avoid** (interactions, alcohol, etc.)

6. **Simple reminder** - one key takeaway sentence

FORMAT: Use bullet points, large font suggestions, simple words
INCLUDE: Suggestion for a medication reminder system
```

## 3. Procedure Preparation

```
PROCEDURE: Colonoscopy
PATIENT: First-time, anxious about the procedure

Create patient preparation guide:

**BEFORE THE PROCEDURE**
- Timeline: What to do when (day before, morning of)
- Diet instructions: Clear, specific examples
- Bowel prep: Step-by-step, managing common issues

**DAY OF PROCEDURE**
- What to bring
- What to expect at check-in
- The procedure itself (honest but reassuring)
- Duration

**AFTER THE PROCEDURE**
- Recovery: What's normal
- When you can eat/drive/work
- Results: When and how

**ADDRESSING COMMON FEARS**
- "Will it hurt?" 
- "Will I be awake?"
- "What if they find something?"

TONE: Reassuring but honest
INCLUDE: Encourage them to write down questions for the doctor
```

## 4. Lifestyle Modification Guidance

```
CONTEXT: Patient just diagnosed with hypertension
GOAL: Create actionable lifestyle guidance

Structure the advice as:

**START HERE** (Week 1-2):
- One simple dietary change
- One activity to add
- One thing to track

**BUILD ON SUCCESS** (Week 3-4):
- Next steps if first changes are working
- How to measure progress

**LONG-TERM HABITS** (Month 2+):
- Sustainable changes
- How to handle setbacks

**PRACTICAL TIPS**:
- Grocery shopping suggestions
- Restaurant ordering tips
- Exercise that doesn't require gym
- Stress management techniques

**MOTIVATION**:
- What benefits to expect and when
- Success stories (generic)
- Small wins matter

AVOID: Overwhelming with too many changes at once
INCLUDE: Acknowledge that change is hard
```

## 5. Explaining Test Results

```
TASK: Help patient understand laboratory results

TEST: Complete Blood Count (CBC)
PATIENT VALUES:
- Hemoglobin: 10.2 g/dL (low)
- WBC: 7.5 x10^9/L (normal)
- Platelets: 245 x10^9/L (normal)

Create explanation:

1. **What this test measures** (simple analogy)

2. **Your results explained**:
   - What's normal (green light)
   - What needs attention (yellow light)
   - What's concerning (red light)

3. **What the low hemoglobin means**:
   - Possible causes (non-scary)
   - Common symptoms
   - Why we need to investigate

4. **Next steps**:
   - Additional tests that might be ordered
   - What you can do now
   - When to follow up

5. **Questions to ask your doctor**:
   - Suggested list

AVOID: Making the patient panic
INCLUDE: "Most cases of anemia are easily treated"
```

## 6. Norwegian Language Patient Materials

```

TILSTAND: Hjertesvikt (Heart Failure)

LAG PASIENTBROSJYRE SOM INKLUDERER:

- Enkel forklaring uten medisinske ord
- Bruk sammenligning/analogi

- Dagligdagse beskrivelser
- Når bør du kontakte lege

- Hvorfor de er viktige
- Vanlige bivirkninger
- Ikke slutt uten å snakke med lege

**LIVSSTILSTIPS**
- Salt og væske
- Aktivitet
- Vekt - hvorfor og hvordan følge med

**PRAKTISK HJELP**
- Hjelpemidler som finnes

- Hvor få mer informasjon

SPRÅKNIVÅ: Enkelt, klart norsk
UNNGÅ: Faguttrykk, lange setninger
INKLUDER: Oppmuntrende budskap om livskvalitet
```

## 7. Pediatric Parent Education

```
CONTEXT: Explaining a child's diagnosis to parents

DIAGNOSIS: Asthma in a 6-year-old
AUDIENCE: Parents, first-time dealing with chronic condition

Create parent guide:

**UNDERSTANDING ASTHMA**
- What's happening in your child's lungs (child-friendly analogy)
- This is manageable - many kids with asthma live normal lives

**DAILY MANAGEMENT**
- Medications explained (preventer vs. reliever)
- Inhaler technique (step by step)
- Avoiding triggers at home and school

**AT SCHOOL**
- Action plan for teachers
- Sports participation
- What to tell your child's friends

**EMERGENCY SITUATIONS**
- How to recognize an asthma attack
- Step-by-step what to do
- When to call emergency services

**EMOTIONAL SUPPORT**
- Helping your child cope
- Not making them feel "different"
- Building independence as they grow

INCLUDE: Resources for Norwegian asthma support groups
```

## 8. Shared Decision-Making Aid

```
SCENARIO: Treatment options discussion
CONDITION: Early-stage prostate cancer

Create a decision aid for patient and family:

**YOUR OPTIONS**

| Option | What it involves | Potential benefits | Potential risks |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Active surveillance | | | |
| Surgery | | | |
| Radiation | | | |

**QUESTIONS TO CONSIDER**
- What matters most to you?
- How do you feel about the trade-offs?
- What are your concerns about each option?

**WHAT RESEARCH SHOWS**
- Outcomes comparison (simple terms)
- Quality of life considerations

**THERE'S NO WRONG ANSWER**
- Different choices suit different people
- Take time if you need it
- You can ask more questions

**PREPARING FOR YOUR NEXT APPOINTMENT**
- Write down your questions
- Bring a family member if helpful
- It's okay to ask for more time
```
