# Project Pitch & Panel Q&A - Prompt Templates

Templates for preparing compelling project presentations and handling challenging questions from evaluation panels. Designed for the Team Project: **Precision Medicine and Quantitative Imaging in Glioblastoma**.

> **IMPORTANT**: Use these templates to prepare, but practice your pitch with real humans. AI can help you anticipate questions, but confidence comes from practice.

---

## PART 1: CRAFTING YOUR PITCH

### 1. The Elevator Pitch (30-60 seconds)

```
TASK: Create a compelling elevator pitch

PROJECT: [Your project title]

STRUCTURE (30 seconds = ~75 words):

**HOOK** (1 sentence)
- Start with a striking fact or question
- Example: "Every day in Norway, one person is diagnosed with glioblastoma—the deadliest brain cancer."

**PROBLEM** (1 sentence)
- What's broken/missing
- Example: "Current diagnosis requires invasive biopsy and weeks of waiting."

**SOLUTION** (1-2 sentences)
- Your approach
- Example: "We're developing AI that predicts tumor characteristics from MRI scans alone."

**IMPACT** (1 sentence)
- Why it matters
- Example: "This could cut diagnosis time from weeks to hours and spare patients unnecessary surgery."

**ASK/CLOSE** (1 sentence)
- What you need or next step
- Example: "We're seeking collaborators to validate our approach across Norwegian hospitals."

GENERATE 3 VERSIONS:
1. Technical audience (more detail)
2. General audience (no jargon)
3. Patient/advocate audience (human impact focus)
```

---

### 2. The 3-Minute Pitch

```
TASK: Structure a 3-minute project pitch

FORMAT: Approximately 450 words, clear sections

**MINUTE 1: THE PROBLEM**
- Hook: Attention-grabbing opening
- Context: Brief background (glioblastoma, current standard of care)
- Pain point: What's not working
- Stakes: Why this matters NOW

**MINUTE 2: YOUR SOLUTION**
- Core innovation: What makes your approach different
- How it works: Brief, accessible explanation
- Preliminary evidence: What supports feasibility
- Team capability: Why you can deliver

**MINUTE 3: IMPACT & ASK**
- Expected outcomes: What success looks like
- Timeline: Key milestones
- Resources needed: What you're asking for
- Vision: The bigger picture if this succeeds

STYLE GUIDANCE:
- One clear message per section
- Avoid jargon; explain technical terms
- Use concrete examples and numbers
- End with a memorable closing statement
```

### 3. The 10-Minute Presentation

```
TASK: Create slide-by-slide outline for 10-minute presentation

PROJECT: Precision Medicine and Quantitative Imaging in Glioblastoma

SLIDE STRUCTURE:

**SLIDE 1: TITLE** (15 sec)
- Project name
- Team members
- Date/context

**SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM** (60 sec)

- Current diagnostic pathway
- Key limitation/gap
- Visual: Survival curve or patient journey

**SLIDE 3: WHY NOW?** (45 sec)

**SLIDE 4: OUR APPROACH** (90 sec)
- Overview of methodology
- Key innovation
- Visual: Pipeline diagram
- Differentiation from existing work

**SLIDE 5: PRELIMINARY RESULTS** (60 sec)
- What you've done
- Initial findings
- Visual: Key figure/result

**SLIDE 6: METHODS DETAIL** (60 sec)
- Technical approach
- Data sources
- Analysis plan
- Validation strategy

**SLIDE 7: TEAM & EXPERTISE** (30 sec)
- Who does what
- Relevant experience
- Collaborators

**SLIDE 8: TIMELINE & MILESTONES** (30 sec)
- Key phases
- Deliverables
- Dependencies

**SLIDE 9: IMPACT & SIGNIFICANCE** (45 sec)
- Clinical translation potential
- Patient benefit
- Broader implications

**SLIDE 10: ASK & SUMMARY** (45 sec)
- What you need
- Key takeaways (3 points)
- Contact information
- Closing statement

TOTAL: ~10 minutes (leave 5+ for Q&A)
```

## PART 2: HANDLING PANEL QUESTIONS

### 5. Anticipating Tough Questions

```
ROLE: You are a skeptical grant reviewer/evaluation panel member.

PROJECT: [Brief description of the project]

Generate challenging questions in these categories:

**SCIENTIFIC RIGOR** (5 questions)
- Methodology concerns
- Statistical validity
- Reproducibility issues
- Alternative explanations

**FEASIBILITY** (5 questions)
- Timeline realism
- Resource adequacy
- Technical risks
- Team capability

**SIGNIFICANCE/IMPACT** (5 questions)
- Clinical relevance
- Incremental vs. transformative
- Competition/alternatives
- Translation pathway

**ETHICS & BROADER IMPACTS** (3 questions)
- Patient safety
- Equity concerns
- Unintended consequences

**CURVEBALL QUESTIONS** (3 questions)
- Questions you haven't anticipated
- Edge cases
- "What if X fails?"

For each question, rate:
- Likelihood (1-5)
- Difficulty (1-5)
- Priority to prepare (1-5)
```

---

### 6. Structuring Your Answers

```
FRAMEWORK: The STAR-I Method for Panel Questions

**S - SITUATION** (brief context)
"That's an excellent question about our validation approach..."

**T - TASK/CHALLENGE** (acknowledge the concern)
"You're right that external validation is critical for clinical translation..."

**A - ACTION** (what you're doing)
"We've designed a three-phase validation strategy..."

**R - RESULT** (expected outcome)
"This will give us performance data across multiple patient populations..."

**I - INSIGHT** (deeper understanding)
"What we've learned from preliminary work suggests..."

TIMING:
- 30-60 seconds for simple questions
- 90-120 seconds for complex questions
- Don't ramble; it's okay to be concise
```

### 7. Question-Specific Templates

#### "Why should we fund you instead of others?"

```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:

1. **ACKNOWLEDGE COMPETITION** (briefly)
   "There are several excellent groups working on brain tumor AI..."

2. **UNIQUE STRENGTHS** (2-3 points)
   - What you do differently
   - Team expertise
   - Unique resources/data access

3. **COMPLEMENTARITY** (if appropriate)
   "Our work complements these efforts by focusing on..."

4. **TRACK RECORD** (briefly)
   "Our team has demonstrated [relevant achievement]..."

5. **CONCRETE DELIVERABLES**
   "Within [timeframe], we will deliver [specific outputs]..."
```

#### "What if your main hypothesis is wrong?"

```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:

1. **ACKNOWLEDGE UNCERTAINTY**
   "Science involves uncertainty, and we've built our approach with that in mind..."

2. **ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESES**
   "If our primary hypothesis is incorrect, the data will inform alternatives..."

3. **VALUABLE OUTPUTS REGARDLESS**
   "Even if the main hypothesis fails, we will have generated [valuable assets]..."

4. **PIVOT STRATEGY**
   "Our modular approach allows us to pivot to [alternative direction]..."

5. **SCIENTIFIC VALUE**
   "Negative results in this case would still advance the field by..."
```

#### "Your timeline seems ambitious—is this realistic?"

```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:

1. **SHOW YOU'VE THOUGHT ABOUT IT**
   "We've carefully considered the timeline based on our experience..."

2. **MILESTONE RATIONALE**
   "Each milestone is based on [specific estimates/prior work]..."

3. **RISK MITIGATION**
   "We've built in [X weeks/months] buffer for unexpected challenges..."

4. **PARALLELIZATION**
   "Several tasks can proceed in parallel, including..."

5. **CONTINGENCY**
   "If [specific phase] takes longer, we can [specific adjustment]..."

6. **TRACK RECORD** (briefly)
   "Our team has delivered on similar timelines in [prior project]..."
```

#### "How will you handle the ethical concerns?"

```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:

1. **ACKNOWLEDGE IMPORTANCE**
   "Ethics is central to our approach, not an afterthought..."

2. **SPECIFIC MEASURES**
   - Data privacy: "All patient data is anonymized using..."
   - Consent: "Our consent process includes..."
   - Bias: "We're actively testing for bias across..."
   - Oversight: "We have established [ethics board/review process]..."

3. **ONGOING VIGILANCE**
   "We'll continue monitoring for [specific concerns] throughout..."

4. **STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT**
   "We're involving [patients/clinicians/ethicists] in..."
```

#### "How is this different from [competitor/existing approach]?"

```
ANSWER STRUCTURE:

1. **ACKNOWLEDGE THEIR WORK**
   "The work by [group/approach] is excellent and has shown..."

2. **KEY DIFFERENTIATORS** (be specific)
   - Methodological: "Our approach differs in..."
   - Data: "We have access to..."
   - Scope: "We focus specifically on..."
   - Integration: "We combine X with Y, which hasn't been done..."

3. **COMPLEMENTARITY**
   "Our work could potentially complement theirs by..."

4. **AVOID NEGATIVITY**
   - Don't criticize competitors
   - Focus on what you add, not what they lack
```

### 8. Handling Difficult Situations

#### When You Don't Know the Answer

```
TEMPLATES:

HONEST ADMISSION:
"That's an excellent question, and I don't have the data to answer it 
confidently. What I can say is [related information]. We plan to 
address this in [phase/timeline]."

PARTIAL ANSWER:
"I can speak to part of that question. Regarding [aspect], we know 
[information]. The [other aspect] is something we're still investigating."

REFRAME CONSTRUCTIVELY:
"That's something we hadn't fully considered. My initial thought is 
[reasonable speculation], but we'd need to [specific action] to be certain."

NEVER:
- Make up data
- Bluff technical details
- Get defensive
- Give a rambling non-answer
```

#### When Facing Hostile Questions

```
STRATEGIES:

1. **STAY CALM**
   - Pause before answering
   - Breathe
   - Maintain neutral body language

2. **ACKNOWLEDGE THE CONCERN**
   "That's a valid concern that we take seriously..."

3. **AVOID DEFENSIVENESS**
   - Don't argue with the questioner
   - Stick to facts
   - Be concise

4. **BRIDGE TO STRENGTHS**
   "While [concern] is important, our approach addresses it through..."

5. **IF TRULY UNFAIR**
   - Answer calmly, factually
   - Don't get drawn into debate
   - Move on gracefully
```

#### When Asked the Same Thing Multiple Ways

```
STRATEGIES:

1. **RECOGNIZE THE PATTERN**
   - They may not be satisfied with your answer
   - Or they're testing consistency

2. **PROVIDE ADDITIONAL DETAIL**
   "To expand on my earlier point..."

3. **ASK FOR CLARIFICATION**
   "I want to make sure I'm addressing your concern—could you 
   help me understand which aspect you'd like me to elaborate on?"

4. **OFFER TO FOLLOW UP**
   "I'd be happy to provide additional documentation on this 
   after the presentation."
```

### 11. Final Preparation Checklist

```
BEFORE YOUR PITCH:

**CONTENT**
- [ ] Can you explain the project in 30 seconds?
- [ ] Do you have a clear, memorable key message?
- [ ] Are all claims supported by evidence?
- [ ] Have you anticipated the top 10 questions?

**DELIVERY**
- [ ] Have you practiced the full presentation 3+ times?
- [ ] Is it within time limit with buffer?
- [ ] Have you practiced Q&A with a mock panel?
- [ ] Do all team members know their roles?

**MATERIALS**
- [ ] Backup of slides in multiple formats
- [ ] Key facts/numbers memorized (not read)
- [ ] One-page handout for panel (optional)
- [ ] Contact information ready to share

**MINDSET**
- [ ] You know your material better than anyone
- [ ] The panel wants you to succeed
- [ ] Tough questions = engagement, not attacks
- [ ] It's okay to not know everything

**ON THE DAY**
- [ ] Arrive early
- [ ] Test technology
- [ ] Water available
- [ ] Deep breaths before starting
```
