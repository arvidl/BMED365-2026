# Ethics Case Analysis - Prompt Templates

These templates demonstrate structured approaches to analyzing ethical dilemmas in healthcare AI.

---

## 1. Four Principles Framework Analysis

```
ROLE: You are a medical ethics educator helping students analyze ethical dilemmas.

CASE: A hospital wants to implement an AI system that predicts which patients 
are likely to miss their appointments. The system works well but has been found 
to have higher false-positive rates for patients from lower socioeconomic backgrounds.

Analyze this case using the Four Principles framework:

**AUTONOMY**
- How does this affect patient's right to make informed decisions?
- Is consent required? What should patients be told?
- Can patients opt out?

**BENEFICENCE**
- What benefits could this system provide?
- Who benefits most? Patients? Healthcare system?
- How can benefits be maximized?

**NON-MALEFICENCE**
- What potential harms exist?
- Who might be harmed?
- How can harms be minimized?

**JUSTICE**
- Is the system fair to all patient groups?
- Does it exacerbate existing inequities?
- How should resources be distributed?

**BALANCING THE PRINCIPLES**
- Which principles are in tension?
- How would you prioritize them in this case?
- What would you recommend?
```

## 3. Real-World Case Discussion

```
CASE: The Epic Sepsis Model Controversy

BACKGROUND:
- Epic Systems deployed a sepsis prediction model in hundreds of hospitals
- An external validation study (Wong et al., 2021) found the model performed 
  significantly worse than Epic's reported metrics
- Hospitals had been using this model for clinical decisions

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS:

**TRANSPARENCY**
- What information should AI vendors disclose about their models?
- Who is responsible for independent validation?
- How should performance claims be verified?

**ACCOUNTABILITY**
- Who is responsible when a deployed AI underperforms?
- What are the obligations of vendors? Hospitals? Regulators?
- What recourse do patients have?

**CLINICAL DECISION-MAKING**
- How should clinicians weigh AI recommendations?
- What happens when AI conflicts with clinical judgment?
- How do we maintain clinical skills when using AI?

**LESSONS LEARNED**
- What should change about how AI is deployed in healthcare?
- What regulations might help?
- How can this inform our own AI projects?

REFERENCE: Search for "Wong et al. 2021 Epic sepsis" to read the study
```

## 4. Bias Detection Exercise

```
SCENARIO: You are auditing an AI system before deployment

AI SYSTEM: Chest X-ray analysis for pneumonia detection
TRAINING DATA: 100,000 chest X-rays from a large urban teaching hospital
PERFORMANCE REPORTED: 95% sensitivity, 90% specificity

TASK: Identify potential sources of bias

**DATA COLLECTION BIAS**
- Who was included in the training data?
- Who might be underrepresented?
- What systematic differences might exist?

**LABEL BIAS**
- How were diagnoses confirmed?
- Who labeled the data?
- What gold standard was used?

**MEASUREMENT BIAS**
- Are X-rays from different equipment types?
- Are there quality variations?
- How might this affect AI performance?

**DEPLOYMENT CONTEXT**
- Where will this be used?
- How might the new population differ?
- What performance monitoring is needed?

**MITIGATION STRATEGIES**
- What would you recommend before deployment?
- How should the system be monitored?
- What reporting mechanisms are needed?
```

## 6. Consent and Transparency Scenario

```
SCENARIO: Designing consent for AI-assisted diagnosis

CONTEXT: A hospital is implementing an AI system that will analyze 
all chest X-rays before a radiologist reviews them. The AI highlights 
areas of concern and provides a preliminary assessment.

DESIGN A CONSENT PROCESS:

**INFORMATION TO PROVIDE**
- What should patients know?
- How should it be explained?
- When should this information be provided?

**OPT-OUT OPTIONS**
- Should patients be able to opt out?
- What are the implications of opting out?
- How should opt-out be handled?

**ONGOING COMMUNICATION**
- Should patients be told if AI was used in their care?
- How should this appear in medical records?
- What if AI and human disagree?

**PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION**
- Draft sample consent language (patient-friendly)
- Create a simple information sheet
- Design workflow for obtaining consent

**DISCUSSION**
- Is traditional informed consent appropriate for AI?
- What new concepts might be needed?
- How do we balance information with overwhelming patients?
```

## 7. Future Scenario Analysis

```
SCENARIO: It's 2030 and AI capabilities have advanced significantly.

Consider this hypothetical situation:
An AI system can predict which medical students will become 
high-performing physicians with 85% accuracy based on their 
performance in years 1-2 of medical school.

ANALYZE:

**POTENTIAL USES**
- How might medical schools use this?
- How might residency programs use this?

**ETHICAL CONCERNS**
- Self-fulfilling prophecy effects
- Impact on student wellbeing
- Fairness and bias considerations
- Effect on diversity in medicine

**POLICY QUESTIONS**
- Should such systems be allowed?
- If allowed, how should they be regulated?
- What safeguards would be essential?

**DEEPER QUESTIONS**
- What makes a "good" physician? Can AI measure this?
- Is prediction the same as potential?
- What would we lose if we optimized for predicted performance?

FORMAT: Write this as if preparing for a medical school ethics committee discussion
```

## 8. Personal Reflection Exercise

```
REFLECTION PROMPT:

Consider a future where AI is deeply integrated into healthcare:

1. **Your future practice**
   - How do you imagine AI will affect your daily work?
   - What concerns do you have?
   - What excites you?

2. **Your responsibilities**
   - What ethical obligations will you have regarding AI?
   - How will you maintain your skills?
   - How will you explain AI to patients?

3. **Your role in shaping AI**
   - How can clinicians influence AI development?

4. **Lines you wouldn't cross**

5. **Preparing yourself**
   - What knowledge do you need?

   - How can you stay informed?

WRITE: A 300-500 word personal reflection (for submission or self-assessment)
```

## Discussion Questions for Class

### Small Group Discussion

1. "Should patients have the right to know when AI was involved in their care?"
   - Argue both positions
   - What practical challenges exist?

2. "Who should be liable when AI makes a diagnostic error?"
   - Discuss: physician, hospital, AI developer, or shared?
   - How does this compare to other medical errors?

3. "Is it ethical to deploy AI that performs worse for some groups if it 
   still helps the majority?"
   - Consider utilitarian vs. justice perspectives
   - What threshold of disparity is acceptable?

4. "Should AI be required to explain its decisions in healthcare?"
   - Trade-offs between explainability and performance
   - What level of explanation is sufficient?

### Case-Based Discussion

Present students with a scenario and ask:
- What would you do?
- Why?
- What principles guide your decision?
- What additional information would you want?
